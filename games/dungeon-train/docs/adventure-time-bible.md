# Dungeon Train — Adventure Time research notes

A reference for making the game look, sound and play like *Adventure Time*, and a stockpile of places,
monsters, bosses, weapons and magic items from the show to add later. Everything here comes from the
sources listed at the bottom (mostly the Adventure Time Wiki and Wikipedia). Notes in *italics* are
ideas for the game, not facts from the show.

---

## 1. What the show looks and feels like

### The opening titles
One continuous camera move, about 12 seconds, with no cuts, sweeping across Ooo:

1. Black screen and wind. A dark hill of apocalyptic trash fades in: nuclear bombs, a rat in an old
   TV, a VHS tape (Ooo is Earth, a thousand years after the Mushroom War).
2. A hand pokes out of a tree stump making a wizard sign.
3. The Ice Kingdom: penguins hugging on a hill, a Snow Golem, then the Ice King turning around angry
   and throwing ice magic.
4. The Candy Kingdom: pastel trees, candy citizens (Starchy, Mr. Cupcake, the Gumball Guardians…),
   up a tower through the clouds where Princess Bubblegum rides Lady Rainicorn.
5. Green, somber mountains: Marceline plays bass in the shadow under a ruined highway, then hisses.
6. The Tree Fort with a two-headed duck outside, then inside: Finn and Jake with BMO, a fist bump,
   and the title pose — Finn raising a sword, Jake lounging beside him.

The theme is sung by Pendleton Ward with a ukulele, recorded as a "temporary" track with room noise
(typewriter, floorboards) that they kept. It feels handmade, not polished.

*For the game:* the title screen should feel like that sweep — a camera drifting over little
landmarks of Ooo and landing on the Tree Fort — and the music should be a plucky, lo-fi ukulele-ish
strum, never a big orchestral fanfare.

### The end credits
"Island Song" (a version of "Christmas Island" by Ashley Eriksson). No main characters: a bee, a worm,
a ladybug and two butterflies on a grassy field. Soft, calm, a bit melancholy. The finale reveals the
song is sung by the Music Hole.

*For the game:* the end-of-trip report can borrow this mood — a quiet grassy field with little bugs,
soft evening colors — as a breather after the action.

### Episode title cards
Every episode opens on a **hand-painted title card**: pulp novel covers, Frank Frazetta fantasy
paintings, Dungeons & Dragons art, B-movies, '70s martial-arts posters. Pendleton Ward sketched each on
a Post-it; Phil Rynda drew it up; Paul Linsley (later Joy Ang and others) painted it. Nick Jennings
scanned **vintage paper textures** for the borders and added **dithering** that imitates old comic
book printing.

*For the game:* each trip can open on its own "title card" — an aged-paper frame, a painted-looking
image of the line and its boss, the line's name in chunky hand lettering.

### The logo
Two stacked levels in ALL CAPS: "ADVENTURE" on top with an oversized "A", "TIME" underneath. Dark red
lettering with black accents (glossier and lighter after 2010). A sword runs horizontally through the
letters; its tip makes the middle bar of the "E". Blade light blue and gold, handle chocolate brown
with a bluish gem. It's hand-drawn lettering, not a font: chunky, bubbly, slightly wobbly.

*For the game:* "DUNGEON TRAIN" can follow the same idea without copying it: two stacked lines, a big
first letter, warm red letters with a thick dark outline and a glossy highlight, and Finn's golden
sword through the middle. Close free fonts: Luckiest Guy, Titan One, Chewy.

### Art direction
- **Backgrounds:** Ghostshrimp's "Ghostshrimp World" designs, painted by Sue Mondt and Martin
  Ansolabehere, art-directed by Nick Jennings. Soft, painterly, flat-ish color with texture, and
  ruins of the old world hiding in the scenery (buried cars, cracked highways, old subway stations).
- **"Candyland on the surface, dark underneath."** Pastel candy colors next to decay, bones and
  the Mushroom War's leftovers. Cute and creepy at the same time.
- **Characters:** simple iconic shapes, "noodle arms" (rubber hose), dot eyes, small mouths.
  Influences: Max Fleischer, *Felix the Cat*, Miyazaki's *Totoro*, '80s video games.
- **Tone:** whimsical, weird and funny, with real emotion; relaxed, conversational dialogue.
  Catchphrases: "Mathematical!", "Algebraic!", "Oh my glob", "What the lump", "Rhombus!", "Shmowzow!".

### UI rules taken from all this
1. Warm, bright, handmade. Paper and paint, not glass and neon.
2. Thick dark ink outlines (the show's line color is a deep navy/black) around panels and buttons.
3. Colors from Ooo: grass green, sky blue, candy pink, Jake's orange-yellow, Finn's blue, BMO teal,
   Ice Kingdom blues, lava orange. Dark backgrounds only where the place is dark (dungeons, the
   Nightosphere).
4. Chunky cartoon lettering for titles; a round, friendly font for text; a handwriting font for notes
   and flavor text.
5. Aged-paper frames and a light dither/halftone texture as a nod to the title cards.
6. Little doodles (stars, hearts, sparkles) and bouncy motion, used sparingly.

## 2. The remodeled off-train lines

The optional lines now leave the train. *Game plan for each:*

### The Mystery Dungeon (was the Classic Dungeon line, tier 2)
In "Dungeon Train", Finn and Jake were on their way to a mystery cave Flambo's brother told them about
when the train got in the way. In "Mystery Dungeon" the first room reads: *"Beyond this room are a
thousand rooms, outside of them still more… find the room amidst the rooms that lies exactly
center."* Rooms include a baking room, a steamy pool with a Monster Fish, a tentacle monster, and a
spiral staircase down to an encrypted door.
*Game:* a stone dungeon of connected rooms; the boss room sits in the exact center. Crystal Ants,
Geode Warriors, Mud Monsters and Hair Apes; the Flesh Beast waits at the center.

### Wizard City (tier 4)
Hidden in Wizwamo Canyon behind a magic barrier: it looks like a dead-end ravine until a real wizard
says "Wizards rule!". Red-brown city wall, timber-framed shops marked with secret symbols, cobblestone
streets (a nod to Diagon Alley), the Spell Palace (a stone tower shop run by Ron James), the Wiz Arts
school, and the Grand Master Wizard's round white palace in a walled courtyard. Non-wizards get
arrested by the **Wizard Police** — tall, muscular wizards with a bandana over one eye that fires a
beam turning criminals into objects; they also shoot blasts from their hands. Wizard Prison is a dark
castle over a moat of fire, with floodlights and a single bridge.
*Game:* streets and plazas, market stalls, a tower, the palace courtyard as the boss arena. The
Grand Master Wizard **raises a sky barrier around his arena so nobody can leave**, fires a green ray
from the emerald in his crown, and turns people into cats (he loves cats).

### The Vampire Hive (was the Vampire Cars, tier 6)
From *Stakes*: the Vampire King and his court (the Fool, the Empress, the Hierophant, the Moon). The
Vampire King's castle looks like a giant beehive; the court enjoyed "premium blood decorated with gold
leaf".
- **Vampire King:** lion face, snake tongue, scaly hands, bird feet, white suit, wine-red cape, crown
  like tree branches. Telekinesis (throws people, crushes stakes), levitation, water control, short
  teleports that engulf targets in light, an invisible room. When his essence is pulled out it becomes
  a **cloud monster** — a lion with long spindly legs and snakes for hair. *Phase 3.*
- **The Empress:** black dress, golden snake around her, eyes hidden by a headscarf with a pink gem.
  The snake lifts the scarf and she **hypnotizes** anyone looking at her. *Game: look away when her
  eyes open.*
- **The Hierophant:** shapeshifter (wolf, scorpion tail with poison), old-fashioned: can't enter a house
  uninvited.
- **The Fool:** a vampire gnome with noodly limbs who treats fights like a game of tag; flies.
- **The Moon:** heals from anything, paralyzes with her voice, drops glowing **moon pearls** from a bat
  ear on her forehead; sleeps in an urn on an old pontoon boat and hunts at night.

### The Lich's Well (post-game)
The Lich's lair is an **old subway station from before the Mushroom War**, beyond Iceberg Lake, full of
wrecked subway cars (one with jet engines) and guarded by skeletal guards. It's cold (Princess
Bubblegum knits Finn a sweater for it). Below is the **Well of Power**, in an old train turnaround, fed
by a dripping pipe of toxic waste. Finn brings the Gauntlet of the Hero Billy; the Lich burns it to
dust, possesses Finn, and is beaten by the sweater ("it has the power of liking someone a lot").
*Game:* platforms, tunnels and wrecked cars; the well chamber is the boss arena. A train level
that's off the train.

## 5. Monsters by habitat

- **Candy Kingdom:** candy zombies, Banana Guards, Gumball Guardians, Candy Corn rats.
- **Ice Kingdom:** penguins, Snow Golems, Ice Toads ("ALARM!"), Ice Bulls, the Iceclops, snow monsters,
  Snow-a-constrictors.
- **Fire Kingdom:** Fire Wolves (black fur with glowing cracks; hunt in packs, big ones are mounts),
  Flambits (little fire cats), Flame Guards, Lava Men.
- **Forests and fields:** Hug Wolves (werewolves with crocodile eyes that must hug), Why-wolves
  (werewolves who stop to lecture), gnomes (winged lightning-shooters from Mount Cragdor), goblins,
  Tree Witch, Fruit Witches.
- **Dungeons and ruins:** skeletons, Goo Skulls, Bucket Knights, Hair Apes, Crystal Ants, Geode
  Warriors, Mud Monsters, mimic chests, Demon Eyes, Oozers (post-war mutants drawn to noise), Moldos
  (blobs that eat books), Varmints.
- **Wizard places:** wizards of every kind, Wizard Police, Bufo, Laser Wizard, Forest Wizard.
- **Undead and demons:** vampires, ghosts (Sheet Ghosts, the Gladiator Ghost), Kee-Oth, Demon Cat.
- **Cosmic:** Crystal Guardians (copy your moves; invulnerable while mirrored), Citadel Guardians,
  cosmic criminals from the Citadel (Cosmic Head Devourer, Evil Cosmic Jellyfish, Evil Cosmic UFO…).

## 6. Weapons and magic items

**Finn's swords, in order:** the Golden Sword of Battle ("Scarlet": dented gold blade, black hilt, red
gem), the Root Sword (found on the Mystery Train's caboose; root-twist handle, pinkish blade), the
Demon Blood Sword (family sword from Joshua's dungeon; fire-immune; summons Kee-Oth), the Grass Sword
(cursed, wraps around Finn's arm and extends at will), the Finn Sword (holds a version of Finn inside),
the Small Sword, and the Night Sword (Peppermint Butler's; Nightosphere magic that shackles powers).

**Other weapons from the wiki:** Axe Bass, Blade of the Defender, Candy Cane Shotgun, Cosmic Gauntlets,
Grape Juice Sword, Green Knight's Axe, Gut Grinder's mace, Lightning Sword, Nothung, Shark Sword, Sound
Sword, Spider Wand, the Gauntlet of the Hero (Billy's), the Flaming Sword of Grob Gob Glob Grod,
Finn's crossbows, Peppermint Butler's stake collection.

**Magic items:** the Enchiridion (the hero's handbook), Armor of Zeldron, Bag of Holding, Cyclops
tears, Crystal Gem Apple, Demonic Wishing Eye, Flame Cloak, Future Crystal (shows your future — from
"Dungeon Train"), Glasses of Nerdicon, Ice King's crown, Kiss of Life, Magic Carpet, Nightosphere
Amulet, Power Rings, Soul Stone, Spider Wand, Stone-skin Potion, Time Amulet, Wand of Dispersement,
Wish Orbs, Wizard Robe, Princess Bubblegum's sweater (it has love in it).

*Item ideas:* Scarlet as a starter-legendary sword; the Root Sword from the caboose; the Future
Crystal as a relic that previews the boss; Bubblegum's sweater (immune to possession/fear); the
Gauntlet of the Hero (huge smite, breaks after a few uses); a Bag of Holding (more backpack slots);
Stone-skin Potion snack; Nightosphere Amulet (life steal).

## 7. Episode seeds

- **Dungeon Train** — the train loops forever; loot, boss cars; Finn gets "gray" and wants to stay. A
  Future Crystal shows old Finn still fighting on the train.
- **Mystery Train** — the Root Sword is on the caboose.
- **The Enchiridion!** — trials to win the hero's book.
- **Dungeon** — the Crystal Eye dungeon.
- **Dad's Dungeon** — Joshua's dungeon and the Demon Blood Sword.
- **Mystery Dungeon** — a thousand rooms; find the exact center.
- **Vault of Bones** — Finn takes Flame Princess on a dungeon crawl.
- **The Limit** — the Labyrinth.
- **Belly of the Beast** — a party cave inside a monster's stomach.
- **Varmints** — the Rock Candy Mines.
- **Wizards Only, Fools** — sneaking into Wizard City; Wizard Prison.
- **Wizard Battle** — the Grand Master Wizard's arena tournament.
- **Mortal Folly / Mortal Recoil** — the Lich's lair and the Well of Power.
- **Return to the Nightosphere** — escape from a Nightosphere prison.
- **Thank You / Incendium / Frost & Fire** — Fire and Ice Kingdom action.
- **Crystals Have Power** — the Crystal Dimension.
- **Escape from the Citadel** — the Crystal Citadel and Finn's dad.
- **Stakes** — the vampire court, one vampire at a time.
- **The Silent King** — the Goblin Kingdom.
